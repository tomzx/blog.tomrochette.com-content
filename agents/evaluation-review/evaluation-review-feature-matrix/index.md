This matrix compares the eighteen members of the Evaluation and review category: the harness-engineering platform that judges the work loop rather than outputs (Better Harness), the browser review surface for plans, diffs, running apps, and static HTML (Crit), the pytest-style eval framework, two public harness benchmarks (the vendor-run FrontierHarness Eval and the academic HarnessTax), the receipt-bound completion gate (HermesGate), GitHub's open AI-code-review benchmark (ReviewBench), the typed decision-model eval library (Jevals), two observability and evaluation platforms (Langfuse and Phoenix), the desktop eval workbench where non-engineers rate outputs (Kiln), the replay-based eval platform that re-runs production sessions against your next change (Kitaru), the NeurIPS 2025 D&B benchmark scoring AI research agents over 201 tasks with its rubric-based two-model judge (MLR-Bench), the human annotation surface, the terminal review surface, the PR walkthrough visualizer (PR Lens), the citation-fabrication gate (Verbatim Citation Gate), and the agent-driven local debugger.
The hybrid machine reviewer that used to sit here, OpenCodeReview, moved to the Code review category when this matrix was created, on 2026-08-30.

**The category divides on who judges: the agent itself (Workshop), a metric suite (deepeval), a typed decision model (Jevals), a deterministic zero-token gate ahead of an LLM judge (Verbatim Citation Gate), a receipt-bound check runner ahead of them all (HermesGate), an observability platform's judges (Langfuse, Phoenix), a human (Crit, Plannotator, Hunk, PR Lens), a workbench seeded by human ratings (Kiln), your pinned judgments replayed through calibrated evaluators (Kitaru), a fixed public benchmark's official evaluator (FrontierHarness Eval, HarnessTax, ReviewBench), or a rubric-scored two-model judge panel validated against expert humans (MLR-Bench), and mature teams run more than one column at once.**

Legend: ✓ supported, ✗ not supported, ~ partial or conditional, ? not verified.
Each column links to the full research note; every cell below traces to a source cited there or in the references.

## Reading the matrix

**The CI-gating row is the buy line: deepeval gates merges today, Kitaru turns the cohort that caught a production failure into the gate, HermesGate turns the pre-merge loop's own pass into a checkable receipt, Workshop's absence of CI is its sharpest recorded criticism, and Crit, Plannotator, Hunk, and PR Lens deliberately serve the pre-merge loop instead.**

**The benchmark columns judge a different object than every other column: the systems that review the code or attempt research with it, on public tasks, which makes them evidence for the tool-choice decision; FrontierHarness Eval and ReviewBench are the two columns with a structural conflict of interest, the first's publisher sells agent infrastructure and the second's sells the code reviewer that tops its own leaderboard, HarnessTax and MLR-Bench come from academic authors with no product to sell, ReviewBench's repository is MIT while FrontierHarness Eval's and HarnessTax's carry no license, and MLR-Bench's judge reads execution logs and supplementary code, the one design here built to catch fabricated results.**

**The judge row is the trust line.** An agent judging itself (Workshop) closes loops fastest and has the weakest evidentiary standing; metric suites inherit judge variance; Verbatim Citation Gate's stage 1 and HermesGate's fast and full runners are the only judges here that are neither a model nor a human, a substring check and a digest-bound check runner that cannot be sweet-talked; MLR-Bench's panel is the one judge validated against expert humans in public, with the ten-task sample as the caveat; Crit's, Plannotator's, Hunk's, and PR Lens's humans are the judges whose judgment you do not have to calibrate.
Kitaru's evaluators are the only judges here required to prove agreement with pinned human verdicts before they gate anything, which makes its trust question empirical rather than rhetorical.

**The license row splits the vendors: MIT and Apache-2.0 columns are safe to embed, while Langfuse's proprietary ee/ modules and Phoenix's ELv2 core are the two cells that constrain what a business may do with the code, and Langfuse's is now held by ClickHouse, which acquired the project in January 2026.**

**Nobody here is redundant:** Workshop develops agents, deepeval gates them in CI, Jevals guards them in-loop with decision-model judges, HermesGate attests the completion claims all of those loops produce, Langfuse and Phoenix observe and evaluate them in production, Kitaru re-runs what production already taught them against the next change, Plannotator, Hunk, and PR Lens steer and explain them (browser, terminal, and the pull request itself), with Crit covering the browser slot plus the running app and forge sync, Kiln is where the team's ratings become the eval set, Verbatim Citation Gate checks the citations in their answers, FrontierHarness prices the harness choice itself, HarnessTax cross-examines that price with academic independence, ReviewBench prices the reviewer choice the same way with the vendor-conflict caveat attached, MLR-Bench scores the research agents the automated research category builds, and the maturity row says a realistic stack starts with the mature columns and adds the rest as the workflow demands.
Machine review of the pull requests themselves now lives in the [Code Review Feature Matrix](../../code-review/code-review-feature-matrix/index.md).

The surfaced long tail stays in prose until something clears the bar, and the 2026-10-12 resolutions both stay out: vostride/agent-qa (904 stars as of 2026-10-12) is held on dormancy, last push 2026-08-03, no releases ever, an undetected license (fair-source by topic), and no independent discussion of its self-improving QA-agent mechanism, with admission unblocking on any push or release after 2026-08-03; px0-ai/px0 (1,851 stars as of 2026-10-12, MIT, active, a browser IDE for reviewing AI-generated code with harness handoff) judges nothing and is an environment rather than an evaluation instrument, so it resolves as surfaces-kind alongside that category's editors and review workspaces and is returned for routing there.
The 2026-10-13 resolution joins them: stanford-iris-lab/meta-harness (1,652 stars as of 2026-10-13, MIT, the code behind arXiv 2603.28052) is an outer-loop search over harness code for LLM applications, and it scores candidate harnesses only to pick the next one, so its subject is automated harness engineering rather than any judgment on agent output, which resolves it as harnesses-kind and returns it for routing there.

## Choosing from the matrix

- Want LLM behavior blocking merges in CI: deepeval.
- Want independent-ish cost and quality numbers before picking a harness: FrontierHarness Eval, vendor-run caveat attached.
- Want an independent second opinion on the harness-cost question: HarnessTax, the academic complement to FrontierHarness Eval.
- Want a yardstick for what AI research agents actually complete, and a rerunnable judge for their outputs: MLR-Bench, reading the ten-task fabrication sample first.
- Want a shared yardstick for comparing AI code reviewers before picking one: ReviewBench, reading the publication-gate caveat first.
- Want MIT-licensed production tracing, evaluation, and prompt management in one platform: Langfuse, self-hosted or cloud, open-core edges and ClickHouse ownership accepted.
- Want production-grade tracing and evaluation under your own infrastructure: Phoenix, accepting ELv2.
- Want your next change tested against the production sessions that already caught the failure: Kitaru, replaying with tool calls answered from the recording and the unchanged-replay baseline checked first.
- Want machine review of every pull request: the [Code Review Feature Matrix](../../code-review/code-review-feature-matrix/index.md), where OpenCodeReview moved.
- Want your annotations to steer a live agent session: Plannotator.
- Want browser line comments on plans, diffs, and the running app itself, with comments pushable to GitHub or GitLab: Crit.
- Want a fast, annotated human read of a full changeset in the terminal: Hunk, paired with a judging column since it decides nothing.
- Want the change drawn before you read the diff: PR Lens, accepting that the diagrams are interpretations, not verdicts.
- Want non-engineers building and rating the eval set in a GUI: Kiln, checking the fair-code desktop license first.
- Want fabricated RAG quotes rejected before any model call: Verbatim Citation Gate, copying the two-stage pattern even if you outgrow the four-star library.
- Want the trace-to-fix loop while developing an agent locally: Workshop.
- Want allow/escalate/block guardrails inside the agent loop at decision-model cost: Jevals, calibrated on your own labels first.
- Want a repeatable receipt saying exactly which bytes passed which checks before a human reads the PR: HermesGate, reading its PASS-boundary section first.
