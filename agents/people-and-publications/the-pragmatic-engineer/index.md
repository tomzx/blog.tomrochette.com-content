The Pragmatic Engineer is Gergely Orosz's engineering newsletter that brings an engineer-first, data-grounded lens to how AI is changing software teams, tooling, and hiring.
Facts below verified as of 2026-09-20.

**Where the hands-on voices show you how to use an agent, The Pragmatic Engineer shows you how a whole engineering organization absorbs one, and that org view is what makes it distinct in this category.**

## What it is

A long-running engineering newsletter (and podcast) by Gergely Orosz, author of The Software Engineer's Guidebook.
Its AI coverage is systematic: tooling adoption surveys, printed case studies of teams using coding agents, and interviews with practitioners like Steve Yegge on agent workflows.
It consistently runs the data-gathering (AI tooling surveys) that individual tool blogs cannot, giving it a rare empirical angle on adoption.

## Status

Active and heavily subscribed, with more than 1,100,000 subscribers as of 2026-09-18.
As of 2026-09-18 it runs the AI tooling-for-2026 surveys and current agent-adoption coverage: AI Skills with Matt Pocock (2026-09-17), Inside OpenAI's agentic software factory (2026-09-15), Building Codex with Tibo Sottiaux (2026-09-09), and What is happening with code reviews? (2026-09-08).
A large share of AI-specific content sits behind the paid tier, which funds the survey and interview work.

## Strengths

- The org lens is unique in this category: hiring, tooling budgets, and team structure around agents.
- Data-backed pieces, especially the annual AI tooling surveys, quantify adoption rather than anecdote.
- Interview range is broad and on-topic: Yegge on agent futures, Kent Beck on engineering, context-engineering deep dives.
- High editorial polish and packed post-mortems that survive as references.

## Cautions

- The engineering-leadership focus is weaker on the day-to-day terminal-and-token details of running agents.
- Much of the best AI material is paywalled, so the free tier underrepresents the substance.
- Orosz profits from the newsletter business model itself, which occasionally surfaces in sponsorship and event promotion.

## Pricing

Freemium Substack.
A substantial and growing share of AI-engineering content is paid; the free tier retains enough volume to evaluate the fit.

## Compared to

- [Simon Willison](../simon-willison/index.md): the individual practitioner's daily notes versus the org-and-industry analysis; read both and you get the loop and the company simultaneously.
- [Latent Space](../latent-space/index.md): Latent Space for the AI-native startup and research view, Pragmatic Engineer for the established-engineering-company view.
- [Andrej Karpathy](../andrej-karpathy/index.md): Karpathy sets the technical vocabulary, Orosz documents how enterprises actually land it.

## Bottom line

**Recommended for engineering leaders and senior engineers who need the data- and org-grounded view of how teams adopt agents.**
Not for someone chasing pure hands-on tool detail who resents a paywall.
