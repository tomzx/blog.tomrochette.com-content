Strands Decider 2B is the Strands Agents team's Apache-2.0 decision model, a 2B LoRA-plus-pointer readout on Qwen3.5 that answers choice, score, and yes/no questions with calibrated confidence in one pass, shipped with a pip CLI and positioned as the decision component for agentic workflows built on the Strands Agents SDK.

**This is the wave's second company-built open checkpoint, and the first from a hyperscaler's agent team, and it arrives with the strongest self-run evaluation culture in the category and a third-party record that flipped in one regeneration: the seed tables and calibration contract are published to a standard nobody else matches, the v1 2B sat near the bottom of the only board that had measured it, and the v2 generation's five rows entered in one November 2026 regeneration with the 31B seventh.**

## What it is

A small decision model in this category's standard pattern: a state and typed questions go in, one probability per option comes out, no generated text.
The released checkpoint, `strands-decider-2B-qwen3.5-v1-2610` on Hugging Face, is a rank-48 adapter plus a readout head trained on a 246,678-row mix whose builders describe every source family, drop the 20,000 rows whose terms restrict commercial use, and use an Apache-2.0 Gemma 4 teacher with Claude only as an independent row filter whose text never enters the data.
The v1 line is not one model: the same 2026-10-09 release shipped four Gemma 4 siblings (E2B, E4B, 12B, and a 26B-A4B, each the same pointer head on `google/gemma-4-*-it` bases, every one a three-run soup), with per-model self-run tables reading 180 to 207 right of the 231 JevBench public items and 29.78 to 48.69 against the Decision Index's public suite, and the training configs of all five v1 releases were published under `configs/v1` on 2026-10-16.
The v1 checkpoint is a soup of three training runs (the per-layer mean of three seeds' LoRA updates with stacked heads), anchored to the previous release with a KL term so answers do not drift.
Serving is a pip CLI (`pip install strands-decider`, PyPI 0.4.0 since 2026-11-20) with CPU, CUDA, MPS, and MLX extras, MLX running 1.4 to 1.6x faster than MPS on Apple silicon per the README.
The v2 line, now the project's current version per the README section added 2026-11-23, replaces the v1 pointer head with a token readout: one linear map with a row for each of 255 single-token option codes, read off the hidden state at the last prompt token, on five Gemma 4 instruction-tuned bases from E2B to 31B that read a 32,768-token context and take images.
It comes from strands-labs, the org behind the Strands Agents SDK, an AWS-backed agent framework, and the README positions the model as a natural fit for decisions inside workflows built on that SDK.

The launch post (2026-10-07) drew a 285-point Show HN thread, above the bar most of this wave cleared, and the tone was warmer than most decision-model threads: early commenters reported on-device deployments rather than methodology fights, one running it in a browser extension to filter email.
The self-run evidence is unusually disciplined: evaluation/results.md publishes per-seed tables next to the soup's numbers (JevBench public items 180 of 231 tasks right, Brier 0.280, ECE 0.072, HotpotQA 0.832 never trained on), states that the training-data builders are not yet on `main` so the data cannot be rebuilt from the repository, and dates every measurement.
The headline reliability claim is a confidence contract: on short classification tasks it has never seen, answers at confidence 0.9 or higher are right about 95 percent of the time, with "confirm or ask a person" below that, which is the deployment logic the calibrated-probability columns all promise.
The one independent reading flipped with the board's 2026-11-21 regeneration (346 configurations, fetched 2026-11-22): the v1 line's 2B row (20.72, rank 271 on the 2026-11-19 board) left the Decision Index and five "Strands Decider v2" rows entered in its place, the 31B seventh at 72.12 (the highest Decision Index rank any subject of this category's notes holds, above the vendor's Jev 1.14 at 60.24, seventy-ninth), the 26B-A4B forty-seventh at 66.53, the 12B seventy-first at 61.96, the E4B 113th at 55.93, and the E2B 197th at 41.65; the attribution now rests on three legs, the rows' engine names matching the project's `strands-decider-v2` convention, all five v2 checkpoints published Apache-2.0 and ungated in the StrandsAgents org (created 2026-11-17, only the E2B visible to the 2026-11-22 fetch), and the README's v2 table (added 2026-11-23) reporting the same Decision Index 0.3 values the board rows carry as their public index (31B 71.5 against 71.45, 26B-A4B 69.0 against 68.98, 12B 64.1, E4B 63.2 against 63.19, E2B 57.1 against 57.06), so the runs are the vendor's own submissions with their public component disclosed while the Full scores stay the board's private measurement, and evaluation/results.md still carries no v2 section while the v1 2B's own balanced-skill self-run read 30.82; the same table self-reports JevBench (231 tasks) 87.4 to 93.1, OpenDecisions 77.7 to 92.2, DecisionPivot 23.3 to 84.3, and BEV 74.0 to 84.5 across the five sizes on 24 GB to 80 GB of GPU at 62 to 99 ms median per JevBench question on one H100, all vendor-run; JevBench's method-v1.8 board (re-read 2026-11-24) still carries a ranked row only for the v1 line's 26B-A4B sibling, official #107 of 269 ranked systems at 60.01, and no v2 checkpoint.

## Strengths

- **The evaluation culture is the strongest in the category: per-seed results beside the released soup, a KL anchor to the previous release, held-out sets named with their train-split relationships disclosed, and a calibration contract stated as a number you can act on.**
- Vendor standing is concrete: the Strands Agents SDK is one of the major agent frameworks, and a decision model shipped as its native component is the distribution path the H2O note predicted would crowd the individuals.
- The data mix is documented past what most of the wave publishes, including the commercially restricted rows it removed and the teacher-versus-filter roles of the two LLMs.
- Small enough for CPU and Apple-silicon serving with a pip one-liner, which keeps the deployment story inside processes no GPU touches.

## Cautions

- **The board evidence flipped in one regeneration and rests on vendor-submitted runs: the five v2 rows entered the Decision Index on 2026-11-21 with the 31B seventh at 72.12; the weights are published (all five, Apache-2.0, as of 2026-11-24) and the README's v2 table matches the board rows' public-index values, but the Full scores are the board's private measurement, the repository publishes no v2 per-seed results section, and JevBench has measured no v2 checkpoint, so the v1 line's adverse 20.72 remains the only reading taken under the project's own published seed-level discipline.**
- 600 stars, a checkpoint too new for download counts, and a vendor-submitted board record make this the weakest traction of any admitted entry in this wave; the thread's warmth has not yet become deployments at scale.
- The training configs of all five v1 releases landed under `configs/v1` on 2026-10-16, but the data builders themselves are still not on `main` (evaluation/results.md says so as of 2026-11-24), so the one thing the results tables imply you could check, the data, cannot be rebuilt from the repository yet; v2 ships a training-data card and five configs under `configs/v2` instead.
- The project is tied to the Strands SDK's roadmap, and the README's own framing ("an experimental typed classifier" on PyPI) keeps expectations deliberately low.

## Pricing

Pricing does not apply: Apache-2.0 code and weights, a free PyPI package, and no hosted service.
The cost is your own hardware, and the 2B size keeps that small.

## Compared to

- [Jev](../jev/index.md): the closed original whose published numbers this model's self-run tables use as yardsticks; Jev keeps the latency and the SLA, Strands Decider ships inspectable weights with a calibration contract.
- [Decider](../decider/index.md): the open family with the widest checkpoint range, whose 31B ranks fifty-first on the Decision Index where the Strands v2 31B now ranks seventh; choose Decider for measured accuracy across budgets, Strands Decider for the eval discipline and the SDK tie-in.
- [H2O-Lightning-4B](../h2o-lightning-4b/index.md): the other company-built open checkpoint, measured first on the board that has not measured this one; the two are the test case for whether vendors or individuals populate this category.

## Bottom line

**Recommended for engineers inside the Strands ecosystem who want a local, calibrated decision component with published per-seed evidence, and for anyone studying how a vendor should disclose a training run.**
Not as a measured engine today: the v2 board rows are vendor-submitted (the weights are published, the Full scores are the board's private measurement, and no v2 per-seed results section exists), and the v1 line's own third-party reading was adverse.
The disagreeable claim I will defend: the fair comparison is not with the community fine-tunes but with the vendor APIs, and Strands Decider is the first open artifact whose confidence contract is stated precisely enough to be falsifiable, which is worth more than the forty points of Decision Index score that used to separate it from Jev.
