AI Jason is the YouTube practitioner channel of product designer Jason Zhou, the highest-profile video source for working agent workflows, from context engineering to building production AI apps.
Facts below verified as of 2026-08-30.

**He is the video practitioner to follow because he builds and reverse-engineers the tools himself, which fills the YouTube-speed walkthrough niche none of the other voices here occupy.**

## What it is

A YouTube channel, site, and paid community run by Jason Zhou, a product designer and start-up founder who was Head of Product at Relevance AI and runs the AI Builder Club.
The channel focuses on hands-on AI experiments, coding-agent workflows, context engineering, and building production apps, with notable videos on cutting tokens with tmux, giving coding agents context the right way, and understanding agent loops.
He is a VC-backed start-up founder who says he has shipped production AI agents for 3+ years.

## Status

Active as of 2026-08-30.
The channel reports 230K subscribers and 99 videos.
A third-party analytics site lists 230K subscribers and 9M total views, with uploads averaging about one a month.
The latest upload went out on 2026-08-25, so the roughly monthly cadence holds.
The channel is fully monetized, with past sponsors including Canva, Figma, and Vercel.

## Strengths

- He explains execution-level detail, context engineering and agent loops, that written summaries often hand-wave over.
- He builds and reverse-engineers the tools he covers, so the walkthroughs reflect real use rather than press kits.
- The visual format compresses a working workflow into minutes, which suits busy practitioners.
- The content is design-and-product oriented, which makes it accessible to engineers and non-engineers alike.

## Cautions

- The channel is sponsor-funded and monetized, so viewers should discount tool hype in sponsored or trending segments.
- Content is aimed at a broad, sometimes beginner, audience, so depth is shallower than the frontier-research voices.
- Cadence is roughly monthly and video-specific, so there is no cumulative reference archive the way a blog or book provides.
- Titles and framing lean toward hook-driven optimization, which can oversell incremental tricks.

## Pricing

Free to watch on YouTube.
The AI Builder Club community is paid, and he sells a Claude Code playbook; past sources list Canva, Figma, and Vercel as sponsors.
