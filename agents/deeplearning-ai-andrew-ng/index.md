Andrew Ng's The Batch weekly newsletter and DeepLearning.AI courses are the mainstream educator's view of AI and agentic development, the voice that popularized the four agentic design patterns and published the AI Engineering Skills Map.
Facts below verified as of 2026-09-04.

**He is the single highest-reaching educator in this category: where the others write for practitioners already inside the field, Ng is the on-ramp that also steers what the broader industry thinks agents are.**

## What it is

A weekly newsletter, a course platform, and the personal letters of Andrew Ng, a computer scientist and educator, co-founder of Coursera and founder of DeepLearning.AI, who taught the foundational Machine Learning course to millions.
[The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch) is a free weekly roundup of AI news and analysis with his "Letters".
His [agentic AI course](https://learn.deeplearning.ai/courses/agentic-ai) and [AI Engineering Skills Map](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/tag/deeplearning-ai-news) define the vocabulary many teams use for designing agentic workflows.

## Status

Active and highly influential as of 2026-09-04.
The Batch publishes weekly, with the latest numbered issue (368) on 2026-08-28 and further posts through 2026-09-02, and the AI Engineering Skills Map letter is dated 2026-08-14.
DeepLearning.AI reports courses taught to millions of learners and positions itself as the canonical AI education path.

## Strengths

- The weekly newsletter is a convenient, reasonably complete scan of the AI landscape with an engineering takeaway, not raw news.
- He normalized the four agentic design patterns, reflection, tool use, planning, and multi-agent collaboration, which are now common vocabulary in the field.
- The short courses are vendor-neutral and hands-on, which makes them an effective and low-friction way to learn.
- The AI Engineering Skills Map is a rare, concrete statement of what an AI engineer needs to know in 2026.

## Cautions

- The newsletter and packaging are broadly optimistic, and a reader who wants deep skepticism about AI hype will not get it here.
- Education is also his business, so course promotion and cert marketing ride alongside the editorial content.
- Surveys and short courses are broad on-ramps, not deep dives, and certificates do not substitute for shipped work when hiring.
- His mainstream framing can flatten the messy, tool-specific reality that practitioner sources report firsthand.

## Pricing

The Batch newsletter is free.
DeepLearning.AI short courses are mostly free, while Coursera specializations and certificates are paid ($49/month or $399/year as of 2026-06 review).

## Bottom line

**Recommended as the balanced weekly scan and the best-structured way to pick up agentic skills when you are new to them.**
Not for a practitioner who wants deep technical detail, frontier-labs nuance, or sustained skepticism.
