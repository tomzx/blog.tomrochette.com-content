**Thirty-three loops automate research today, and the row that separates them is not capability but judging: everything with a Lean kernel or an official grader behind it produces checkable artifacts, everything without one produces prose or artifacts an adversarial critic and a human must accept, and the newest columns split between a trained 8B model that is its own only judge, a five-minute keep-or-revert loss curve, a correctness-gated kernel benchmark, a gate set the discovery pass builds itself, a campaign scoreboard of partly self-reported benchmarks, a workshop peer review the authors then withdrew from, a reviewer from a different model family, a debate-and-citation-check pipeline, an LLM-as-judge over live financial data, a bank account, a producer-critic pair, your own git worktrees, a wet lab where people still hold the pipettes, a self-hosted search stack, the open baseline the academic literature keeps citing, a grader daemon the agents are sandboxed away from, a user metric scored at every node of a code tree, logs from GPU-cluster jobs bound to the manuscript, benchmark scores over paper reproductions, a wiki with an LLM reviewer, review scores the projects publish against themselves, a held-out-data merge gate on a persistent hypothesis tree, blind reviews written to disk by a pipeline that stops before the paper, an enforced budget ledger that rejects unsupported quotes, a promotion gate that replays matched candidate and incumbent trials under a reserved false-promotion budget, and a manuscript whose every number click-traces back to the code and data that produced it.**
Every cell traces to its member note and that note's fetched references.

## How to read it

The judging row is the deciding one, and it repeats a pattern this section tracks in software tooling: outputs are exactly as trustworthy as the verifier behind them.
AlphaProof's 2024 result and Math Inc.'s formalizations carry kernel-level guarantees; the Anthropic results add named human reviewers on top of the kernel.
Aristotle's headline claims are real where Lean checked them and contested where only the vendor graded them.
OpenAI's two entries are prose-only loops: Deep Research cites, the science program claims, and neither has a machine judge.
Pion is the first proprietary column and the only one whose output is neither artifact nor prose but money: its agents run real businesses, its judge is a bank account plus the operator's own monitoring, and its launch thread's contradiction (the operator calling autonomous resource acquisition the most troubling capability while releasing exactly that) is recorded in its note.
Agon is the first column whose judge is a critic agent on a fresh context rather than a kernel, a grader, or a full-time operator, and its paper's own taxonomy names the failure classes that oracle cannot see, which makes it the cheapest loop to run and the least verified.
OpenResearch is the first column whose loop runs on the reader's own machine: its judge is the evidence tree it archives rather than any external oracle, which makes it the only column where the operator, the compute, and the audit trail are yours.
FutureHouse Robin is the first column aimed at a wet lab rather than mathematics, code, or money: its artifacts are peer-reviewed cell-culture results, its judge is Nature's reviewers plus the assay itself, and its paper shows both the loop's power, a control run where Deep Research scored zero hits, and its boundary, people running every experiment.
Local Deep Research is the first column that hands the reader the whole literature tier: the loop, the engines, and the models run on your machine, and its benchmark table carries its own contamination caveats, which puts it at OpenResearch's self-hosted end of the spectrum rather than Deep Research's product tier.
GPT Researcher is the column the others get measured against: it predates the product tier, its judge is the same no-machine-oracle human read as Deep Research's, and its distinction is being the baseline 150-plus papers cite, which makes it the least novel loop here and the most cross-checked one.
Karpathy Autoresearch is the founding artifact the autoresearch family is named after: its judge is the one oracle here no vendor or model mediates, the validation curve that keeps or discards every experiment, and the scaling thread's critiques (automated hyperparameter tuning, guided brute force) attack its ambition rather than its results.
DeepAnalyze is the first column whose loop is carried by a trained model rather than a prompted harness: an 8B model plans, acts, and judges its own data analyses in one package, which makes it the cheapest loop to run, the only one with no external verifier anywhere, and the one whose self-reported benchmark claims deserve the most discount.
Dexter is the first column aimed at finance: its loop plans and re-checks its own data gathering over paid statement APIs, and its judge is an LLM-as-judge plus the human, which puts its answers at Deep Research's discount with a scratchpad audit trail as partial compensation.
ARIS is the first column whose reviewer must come from a different model family: its report names the category's shared failure mode, the plausible unsupported success, and answers it with cross-model gates rather than a kernel, which places it beside Agon as judge-by-critic while sharing DeepAnalyze's thin independent evaluation.
AutoResearchClaw is the idea-to-paper pipeline that makes citation fabrication a first-class problem: its four-layer citation check and Pivot/Refine failure loops attack the two failure classes the Lean columns never face, wrong sources and silent breakdowns, while its 54.7 percent benchmark claim remains its own.
Kapso is the first column whose judge is a campaign scoreboard: its own evaluator decides every step against an objective declared in advance, and the published check comes from benchmarks, two of which (MLE-Bench and ALE-Bench) the project runs itself while the RelBench leaderboard is self-reported by design, so its claims carry DeepAnalyze's self-report discount with the mitigating detail that the evaluator precedes the run.
Sakana AI Scientist is the ancestor the idea-to-paper columns measure themselves against: its headline first passed a workshop's peer review, and its own write-up supplies the discount (humans picked the three submissions, the paper was withdrawn by design, and none reached the authors' own conference bar), which makes it both the founding artifact and the category's clearest lesson in reading a milestone's fine print.
Agent Laboratory is the human-assist ancestor: its own evaluation is the family's most quotable negative result, ten PhD reviewers scoring its papers 3.5 to 4.0 of 10 against the 5.9 accepted-NeurIPS average, while its AgentRxiv follow-up showed labs sharing papers beat isolated labs on MATH-500, which makes it the citation for both the field's ceiling and the value of shared memory.
AIDE ML is the tree-search ancestor the metric-judged columns descend from: its draft-debug-improve nodes over a user metric are the mechanism Kapso tree-searches and Weco productizes, and its MLE-Bench medal count comes from Agent Laboratory's comparison, one column scoring another.
AI-Researcher is the idea-to-paper column with the largest community and the least license: 5,802 stars against a repository with no license file, dormant since October 2025, its momentum moved into the Novix product, and its judge is the team's own Scientist-Bench.
AiScientist is the only column whose unit of work is the reproduction: its paper track drives a published paper's full reproduction loop on PaperBench while its File-as-Bus protocol makes the workspace, not the chat, the system of record, the coordination variant the other multi-agent columns leave implicit.
AutoSci is the first idea-to-paper column adapted beyond one harness: stable on Claude Code with Codex and OpenCode previews, its SciMem memory persists across projects the way Kapso's hub does, and its reviewer is an MCP-configured model rather than an oracle.
CORAL is the first column whose loop is an agent organization: islands of coding agents in isolated worktrees, a grader daemon the agents are sandboxed away from reading, and shared memory through .coral/public, which makes guarding the judge from the judged an explicit design constraint instead of a hope.
NanoResearch is the idea-to-paper column that binds every number to a run: it submits its own code to local or SLURM GPU clusters and writes only from the returned logs, the self-published answer to the fabricated-results failure the citation checkers cannot see.
Arbor is the first column whose judge is withheld data: gains survive only if they clear a margin on a held-out split the executors never saw, which makes it CORAL's closest cousin with the anti-overfit gate made explicit.
EvoMap AutoResearch is the idea-to-paper column that stops before the paper: its deliverable is an evidence package of runs, critic reports, and blind reviews, and its launch carried the category's first provenance incident, a forged commit shown as Karpathy's during a 3,278-star week.
Mole is the first column with the run's cost inside the loop's contract: a reserve-settle ledger stops the run at the ceiling you set and verbatim-quote checks drop claims the source never said, enforcement the prose-only columns leave to the human.
AutoContext is the column that makes promotion receipts the deliverable: its coach and architect output stays an immutable candidate until matched candidate/incumbent trials, a held-out lane, a field-for-field replay, and an optional audit promote it under a campaign false-promotion budget, which makes it the member whose every kept lesson arrives with the evidence that kept it.
data-to-paper is the column whose judge is built into the artifact: every number, method, and dataset reference in its manuscripts is programmatically chained back through the analysis code to the data, and NEJM AI's peer review then sampled that chain, which makes it the only member whose output carries its own audit trail, with the boundary its launch thread named, hypotheses generated from the same data the paper publishes, being the one thing chaining cannot verify.
AutoKernel is the column whose oracle is a fixed benchmark on one file: the five-stage correctness harness gates every speedup the keep-or-revert loop records, which gives this domain-bound port of Karpathy's loop the community tier's hardest judge, while its headline numbers stay vendor-measured against PyTorch eager and torch.compile rather than CUTLASS or Ansor, the comparison its launch thread demanded and never got.
Evo is the column whose judge is built before the loop runs: the discover pass instruments the benchmark and attaches a held-out score-floor gate automatically, which makes it the generic-codebase sibling of AutoKernel's fixed bench.py, with the discovery step as the new trust surface, a judge the same system that optimizes against it wrote.
The Millennium column is uniformly no: nothing here has solved one, the closest engagement is a failed-but-productive Riemann attempt and a disputed Navier-Stokes claim, and FrontierMath's problems are explicitly built below Millennium scale.
