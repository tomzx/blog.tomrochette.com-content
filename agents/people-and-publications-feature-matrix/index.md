Everything below was verified against live primary sources on 2026-08-29.

**The axis that actually segments the field is focus, not platform: hands-on tool practice, industry-and-org analysis, and frontier-research vocabulary each attract a different reader, and the personalities span all three.**

## Reading the matrix

I read this table by columns, matching a reader slot rather than a source.
**Nobody covers all three focus bands well, which is the argument for following more than one: pick a practitioner, an industry voice, and a vocabulary-setter.**

**The hands-on pair, Willison and Yegge, both build what they write about, but they occupy opposite ends of temperament:** Willison is the reliable daily chronicler, Yegge is the provocative thesis-builder, and their shared builder-grounding is what makes each believable.

**Latent Space and The Pragmatic Engineer are the two industry synthesizers, split by audience:** Latent Space points at the frontier labs and the AI-native startups, The Pragmatic Engineer points at established engineering organizations, so your employer's profile picks your primary.

**Karpathy is the odd one out by cadence and by function:** he publishes rarely but sets the vocabulary (Software 3.0, agentic engineering) that the other four then operate inside, which is exactly why a low-frequency source still anchors the category.

**The missing cell worth naming is an enterprise hands-on voice:** none of these five is the everyday terminal operator inside a large, non-AI company, and that gap is the scaffold a future member should fill.

## Choosing from the matrix

- Need daily, hands-on signal on tools and models: Simon Willison.
- Need the industry, lab, and community view plus events: Latent Space.
- Lead an engineering org adopting agents and want data: The Pragmatic Engineer.
- Want a sharp, opinionated thesis and are willing to cross-check: Steve Yegge.
- Want the durable conceptual framing behind the churn: Andrej Karpathy.
